Silent Skies (senior capstone, 2025–2026)

A team of seven built a modular RC aircraft to test passive and active noise reduction, with a goal of cutting cruise noise by at least 3 dBA. I was an engineer on the controls team, working on the electronics, the circuit integration, and the active noise cancellation (ANC) hardware.

What I did

  • Researched the components and every connection in the circuit, co-wrote the system schematic with my fellow controls engineer, and helped build the circuit from it.
  • Researched feedback, feed-forward, and hybrid ANC. Worked out the target frequency band from motor kV, pack voltage, and blade count: the theoretical maximum tone is about 400 Hz, and the real tone at 60% throttle was about 288 Hz.
  • Chose the battery, ESC/BEC, and GPS using weighted decision matrices. I sized the 8000 mAh battery against early power estimates that turned out to be much higher than reality. The bench test measured 13 A at 70% throttle, about 37 minutes of theoretical endurance against a 15-minute requirement.
  • Selected and integrated the microphone, amplifier, and speaker chain.
  • Used AI-accelerated coding (mostly Gemini 2.5 Pro) to run rapid ANC experiments, testing each implementation on the hardware and revising the code.

How the ANC ended up

We planned a hybrid system with an adaptive LMS filter. Audio feedback loops, latency, and algorithmic complexity made that unworkable, so we switched to feed-forward. A Teensy 4.1 reads the reference microphone, tracks the propeller tone with a bandpass filter and YIN pitch detection (188–388 Hz window), and plays a sine wave at that frequency through the speaker while sweeping through a range of phase shifts. The second microphone logs to an SD card so the best phase shift can be identified afterward.

Two hardware problems along the way: the first GPS never arrived, so I re-ran the selection with shipping time weighted heavily and switched to the Adafruit Ultimate GPS. The lightweight 2-inch speaker was too quiet to cancel a propeller. The prop bench test measured lower power draw than expected, which left the updated power and mass budgets enough room to upsize the speaker.

Results

In a ground test with the motor at about 60% throttle, the best phase setting (10%) reduced sound pressure level by roughly 5 dB compared to the no-ANC baseline. That is SPL, not A-weighted, and we could not repeat it because the hardware was damaged right after the test. None of the three aircraft versions stayed in the air for more than about a second before crashing, so we never got any in-flight noise data.
